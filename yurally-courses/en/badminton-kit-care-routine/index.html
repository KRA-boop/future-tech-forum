Organizing Your Bag and Maintaining Your Equipment: A Checklist for Before and After Practice

G06 · Beginner · Approx. 20 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Be able to establish a routine for putting away equipment after each shot.

Court Practice

  1. Create a post-game checklist. After your match, separate your wet clothes, shoes, and racket, and once you get home, take out the items that need to air out.
  2. Qualification Criteria Complete the process twice in a row using the checklist; do not mix wet clothes and rackets.

Class Notes · Practice and Observations

Incorporate your shoes, grip, strings, and bag into your regular organization routine to make your next practice go more smoothly.

Check Your Readiness Before Practicing

Before heading out, check your racket, shoes, water, and a change of clothes. If the racket frame has visible cracks or is warped, stop using it immediately and have it inspected by a store; do not continue playing if the strings are broken. If the grip is too wet, the surface is peeling, or you can no longer hold the racket securely, address the issue beforehand to avoid discovering the problem on the court.

Key Points for Checking Your Shoes

Check whether there is dirt on the soles of your shoes, whether the laces are tied securely, and whether the upper and sole are visibly separated. If the court is damp or the soles are in poor condition, address the issue first before starting your activity. When determining whether shoes are suitable, consider how well they support your feet during movement, their lateral stability, and how they feel to you; do not base your decision solely on appearance or brand.

Store Equipment Separately After Practice

After your workout, separate your wet clothes, towels, and shoes from the other items in your racket bag. When you get home, open the bag and let your gear dry in a well-ventilated area, then clean it according to the product instructions. Do not leave wet items sealed inside the bag for long periods, and avoid leaving your racket and shoes in a car exposed to high temperatures.

Daily Log of Grip and String Tension

Keep track of when you change the grip and restring your racket, as well as how the racket feels after use. To determine whether the grip needs replacing, check its sweat-absorbing ability, grip, and how securely it’s attached; for the strings, pay attention to wear and any changes in the feel of your shots. Since usage frequency and playing conditions vary greatly, there’s no single replacement schedule that works for everyone.

Wrapping Up in Two Minutes

We recommend that before leaving the court each time, you wipe down your equipment, sort it, and double-check that nothing is missing, then let it air out at home. Making maintenance a regular routine is easier to stick with than doing a major cleanup once in a while. If you share equipment with other players, be sure to check the size and condition first, clean it after use, and return it to its original place.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can You Use a Hair Dryer to Dry Them? Always follow the manufacturer’s instructions; generally, opt for air drying first and avoid exposing them to direct high heat. Does a brand-new sole guarantee safety? You should still check the condition of the material, the court surface, and the actual grip. Self-check

Your shoes and clothes are soaked after a match—what should you do first when leaving the court?

Store wet clothing, shoes, and rackets separately. Once you return to a well-ventilated area, remove the wet items and follow the cleaning and drying instructions for each piece of equipment.
