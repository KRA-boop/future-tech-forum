Positioning for Serves and Returns in Doubles: Decide on Your Strategy for the First Three Shots

B06 · Advanced · Approx. 30 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Ability to coordinate with your partner to divide up the court during the first three shots.

Court Practice

  1. Start with a fixed short serve, then practice 10 sets of the first three shots, alternating between returning the serve down the middle and dropping it at the net.
  2. Qualification Criteria Complete at least 7 sets where students can identify who will return the next shot and who will cover the remaining areas, and then practice these on the court.

First, Understand This Topic

Your serve in doubles will influence the first three shots of the rally. As you prepare to serve, quickly assess the receiver’s position, your partner’s position, and your intended landing spot. When serving a short shuttle, control its height and depth to avoid letting it float above the net; when serving near the body or to the backcourt, have a clear purpose—don’t just aim to get the shuttle over the net. Maintain a consistent serving motion so you can compare the effects of different landing spots.

Practice and Selection Methods

The receiver doesn’t have to force a smash on every shot. When the shuttle is high and in a comfortable position, you can push it into open space or drive it down the middle; when the shuttle is low and close to your body, it’s often safer to first lift it steadily to the backcourt. Coordinate your positioning with your partner to avoid both of you chasing the same shuttle. Immediately after returning the serve, watch your opponent’s racket face and the direction of the return to prepare to block at the net or cover the backcourt.

Note a Detail You Can Adjust

During practice, players can take turns serving short shots or serving near the body, then instruct the receiver to return the shot using one of three techniques—a push, a flick, or a lift—while a third player responds. Set only one objective per set; for example, after a short serve, push the shuttlecock to the center of the court. Avoid changing footwork, speed, and placement all at once. Afterward, discuss which type of return is most easily blocked, then adjust your ready position. For more basic techniques, see A Special Feature on Serves and Return of Serve。

Self-check

When the receiver pushes the shuttlecock between the two players, how can the server avoid rushing to hit it?

Agree in advance on who has priority in the middle of the court, and use short commands during the rally to remind your partner. The player closest to the shuttlecock handles the shot, while the other covers any open areas.
