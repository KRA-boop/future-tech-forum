M03 | Reading Singles Shot Patterns and Rhythm

YouTube Tutorial Videos

Watch the original video on YouTube

Key Points to Watch: Observe the landing points, depth, and rhythm changes in singles rallies, and identify which shot forces your opponent out of position.

After-Class Exercises: Select 10 rallies. Pause after the third shot in each rally, predict the open spaces you can exploit, then watch the rest of the rally and write down the actual results.

M03 · Advanced · Approx. 30 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Being able to recognize when your opponent has been pulled wide and predict the next possible shot.

Recommended to Learn First M01 Offense and Defense Rotation in Doubles

Technique and Decision-Making

On the first pass, focus only on the landing spot and returning to your position; on the second pass, observe when the player accelerates. Deep shots, drop shots, and shots down the middle will alter your opponent’s movement patterns. For the next shot, check whether the shuttle is high enough and whether you’ll reach the spot first. Compare rallies with different scores to avoid judging a strategy based on just one shot.

Court Practice

  1. Find a full replay of a men’s or women’s singles match. Pause the video in the middle of an 8-rally sequence, note the players’ positions, and predict the direction of the next shot and the reason behind it.
  2. Qualification Criteria Be able to list at least three examples of corresponding positions and choices, and explain why one of them was incorrect.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

If you only watch the final shot, you’ll miss how the players created space in the preceding shots. Watch at least from the very first shot of that rally.

Self-check

If you only see a player scoring with smashes, can you conclude that their main strategy is to keep smashing?

First, review how to hit short returns in the previous few rallies, then compare the unsuccessful rallies with the score at that point.

Watch Matches and Select Full Replays

Advanced Practice

Try having you and your partner independently score the same rally—first, each of you write down your prediction, then review the results together. If your judgments differ, go back to the footage right before the player hit the shuttlecock to check whether you overlooked the opponent’s position or the height of the shot.
