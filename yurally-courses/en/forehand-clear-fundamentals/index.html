B10 | Forehand Clear Swing

B10 · Beginner · Approx. 30 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Able to establish a consistent contact point for the backcourt forehand clear.

Recommended to Learn First B03 Six-Point Footwork and Returning to Position

Technique and Decision-Making

Move to the back of the incoming shot, position the contact point above and in front of your body, and maintain a sideways stance and a comfortable swing path. When hitting the shuttlecock, direct it toward the backcourt; after the shot, naturally bring the racket back and return to the ready position. First, practice making successful shots at the same height, then incorporate backward steps.

Court Practice

  1. Start by doing 10 practice swings, then have a partner serve you 2 sets of 8 high serves each. Begin by practicing at half-court distance; once you’ve mastered that, gradually move to the backcourt.
  2. Qualification Criteria Hit 5 out of the last 8 shots to the target depth and be able to return the next shot after getting back into position.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

If you swing your racket only after the shuttlecock has landed behind you, you’re likely to hit it too short or send it floating upward. Start by using slow feeds to find a comfortable contact point.

Self-check

When You Can’t Hit the Shuttlecock Deep Enough from the Backcourt, Should You Focus on Power or Positioning First?

First, observe whether you’re unable to get into position in time or if your contact point is too far back, then gradually adjust the amount of power you apply.

Advanced Practice

Add two steps backward from the center, then hit 8 clear shots, and record how many fewer shots landed in the target zone compared to before. If the success rate drops, determine whether it’s due to a delay in positioning or a misjudged contact point, and in the next round, focus on correcting only one aspect of your technique.
