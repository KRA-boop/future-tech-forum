A04 | Net Play Decision-Making: Dive for the Shuttlecock or Drop Shot

A04 · Advanced · Approx. 25 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

The ability to choose between a smash and a drop shot based on the height of the shot and balance.

Recommended to Learn First A03 Introduction to Net Dives

Technique and Decision-Making

Use a slight downward stroke only when the incoming shot is high and still within your striking range. If the shot is low and you’re sliding forward, first guide the racket gently over the net. For both types of shots, lead with the racket head toward the shuttlecock and make your final adjustment just before contact to avoid revealing your choice too early.

Court Practice

  1. A partner serves a total of 12 net shots—a mix of high and low shots. First, verbally call out “dive” or “drop” before swinging the racket. In the second round, omit the verbal cue.
  2. Qualification Criteria It’s reasonable to aim for at least 9 shots in the final rally, while keeping your body and racket from touching the net.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

When you try to hit a low shot even lower, it’s easy to hit the net. First, use the pause function to review the relative height of the shuttlecock and the net at the moment of contact.

Self-check

When the shuttlecock is served below the net and the racket head is very close to the net, which shot is the most reliable?

Prioritize using a controlled racket face for net shots or clear shots, depending on where you make contact with the shuttlecock and your opponent’s position.

Advanced Practice

After completing the stationary feeding drill, incorporate the same task into a four-stroke drill in half-court. Your partner should focus on only two things: whether you chose the correct shot, and whether you were able to maintain your balance after hitting the shuttlecock. Record the most common mistake for each item once.
