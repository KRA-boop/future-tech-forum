G04 | Grip Thickness and Wrapping Methods

G04 · Beginner · Approx. 20 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Be able to compare the thickness and grip texture of the grip and wrap it properly according to the instructions on the grip.

Recommended to Learn First G01—A Beginner's Guide to Choosing a Racket

Technique and Decision-Making

Start at the bottom of the handle following the product instructions, ensuring the tension and overlap width remain consistent. Once wrapped, check to see if you can easily release and re-grip the racket with your fingers, and make sure the end is secure and free of any protrusions. People who tend to sweat a lot on their hands may want to compare the feel of different materials.

Court Practice

  1. Use the same racket to try out the original grip and the new grip at a slow pace. Compare switching between forehand and backhand strokes, short serves, and flat drives—6 times each.
  2. Qualification Criteria Be able to identify which type is easier to switch and which is less slippery, and check whether the end tape is loose.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

Applying too many layers at once can make it difficult to rotate the racket handle. Adjust one layer at a time to be able to compare the results before and after.

Self-check

The Grip Feels Thick and Stable—So Why Should You Still Practice Switching Between Forehand and Backhand?

Thickness affects the range of motion for your fingers; you should test both how well the racket feels when stationary and how easily you can change your grip while moving.

Advanced Practice

Even grips of the same thickness can offer different levels of sweat absorption and grip. When comparing them, use the same racket and follow the same practice sequence. After the first round, wipe your hands dry, then note the differences you feel after playing a certain number of shots.
