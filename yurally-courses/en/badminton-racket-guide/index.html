How to Choose a Racket? A Detailed Comparison of Weight, Balance Point, and Shaft Stiffness

G01 · Beginner · Approx. 20 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

The ability to compare different racket choices based on controlled swing and return speeds.

Court Practice

  1. Use two rackets with similar settings to practice the same set of serves, clear shots, and consecutive drives, then record the consistency of the latter two.
  2. Qualification Criteria Be able to explain your choice using at least two examples from actual matches.

First, Understand This Topic

There’s no one-size-fits-all answer when it comes to choosing a racket. Players who focus on offense might prefer a racket with a head-heavy feel, while those who need to make quick, consecutive shots may prioritize swing flexibility. Before making a purchase, think about the challenges you face most often: do you struggle to hit backcourt shots with enough efficiency, have trouble keeping up with flat drives, or find your shot direction inconsistent? Starting with your actual challenges makes it easier to find the right choice than simply chasing after popular specifications.

Practice and Selection Methods

Weight class is just the starting point; grip size, balance point, and shaft stiffness also affect how the racket feels in your hand. A stiffer shaft typically requires a faster swing and precise timing; different levels of stiffness provide different feedback, but they all need to complement your playing style. When testing a racket, be sure to compare at least your high clears, flat drives, and defensive reactions. Don’t make a decision after just a few swings, and don’t overlook whether your arm tires easily.

Note a Detail You Can Adjust

If you have the chance to test a racket, try to use the same string and a similar tension. Take note of whether your consecutive flat drives feel smooth, whether you need to exert extra force from the backcourt, and how your arm feels after finishing a game. Change only one factor at a time so you can more easily identify the source of any differences. To determine if a racket is right for you, see if it makes your usual strokes more stable—rather than just relying on the “offensive” or “control” labels on the tag. Self-check

If Both Rackets Send the Shuttlecock Far Enough, How Do You Determine Which One Is Right for You?

Maintain consistency in shot types, feeding the shuttlecock, and the order of shots, then compare the consistency of returns after moving with that of consecutive flat drives. Make your judgment based on actual performance and how the shots feel.
