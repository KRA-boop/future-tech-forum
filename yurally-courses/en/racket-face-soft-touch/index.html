B09 | Racket Face Angle and Light Touch Control

B09 · Beginner · Approx. 25 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Understanding how the angle of the racket face affects the direction of the shot.

Recommended to Learn First B08 Switching Between Forehand and Backhand Grips

Technique and Decision-Making

Start by holding the racket loosely to stabilize the racket head, maintain a consistent height of the incoming shot, and minimize arm movement. In each round, adjust only one factor—either the angle of the racket face or the speed of contact—so you can observe how the landing point changes.

Court Practice

  1. Have a partner toss the shuttlecock from the net—3 sets of 6 shuttles each. In the first set, keep the racket face fixed; in the second set, raise it slightly; in the third set, mix the two landing spots.
  2. Qualification Criteria In the final set, at least 4 shuttlecocks must land in the near or far zone you selected in advance.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

If you try to adjust both the power and the racket angle on every shot, you won’t be able to pinpoint the cause—so first, have your partner keep the height of the serves consistent.

Self-check

How should the serving conditions be set when testing the effect of the racket face?

Maintain similar height, position, and speed, then gradually compare the racket faces.

Advanced Practice

Place two markers each in the near and far zones on the same side of the court. Before feeding the shuttlecock each time, call out the target zone; your partner should only respond with “short,” “on target,” or “long.” After three rounds, start reviewing from the angle you found easiest to predict.
