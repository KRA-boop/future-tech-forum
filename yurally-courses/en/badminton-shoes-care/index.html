Choosing and Caring for Badminton Shoes: Traction, Support, and Safety Are All Key

G05 · Beginner · Approx. 20 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

You can assess the fit and stability of your shoes while moving around the court.

Court Practice

  1. Clear the shuttlecock from a short distance, perform about 3 lateral steps and 3 cross-step stops, and note how your heels slide, your toes push off, and your forefoot presses into the ground.
  2. Qualification Criteria Be able to identify the area that needs the most improvement and explain how to clean and dry the soles of your shoes.

First, Understand This Topic

Badminton shoes should support sudden stops, lateral movements, and frequent starts. When trying them on, wear the socks you normally use for playing to ensure your heel doesn’t slip, there’s enough room for your toes to move, and the upper doesn’t press against the top of your foot. Since your left and right feet may differ slightly, it’s best to try on both shoes. On a safe surface, perform a few small lateral steps and lunges to assess the shoe’s stability. You can decide on the appearance and color last.

Practice and Selection Methods

Traction also depends on the court surface and the cleanliness of the shoe soles. When the sole is covered in dust or grease, traction decreases; once the tread is worn smooth, the shoes may need to be replaced even if the upper is still intact. The soles and support systems of outdoor running shoes are designed for different purposes and are not suitable as direct replacements for indoor sports shoes. If you frequently feel your feet sliding inside the shoes, check the size, laces, and insoles first, rather than simply tightening the laces to solve the problem.

Note a Detail You Can Adjust

After each game, allow sweat and moisture to air out of your shoes; do not leave them sealed in a shoe bag for extended periods. Clean the soles with a slightly damp cloth and let them dry completely before storing. Removable insoles should be taken out periodically to air dry, and the shoe upper should be cleaned according to its material. If the sole is noticeably slipping, the upper is damaged, or the ankle support feels different, don’t just rely on glue repairs to extend the shoe’s life. Information on selection and maintenance is summarized at Categories of Shoes and Maintenance。

Self-check

They feel comfortable when walking straight during a test run, but your heels tend to slip during light side-to-side movements. How would you test them?

Adjust the laces or try a different last, keeping the same socks and performing the same movements. Then compare heel support, forefoot space, and braking feel.
