net kills: Judging Height, Shortening Your Movement, and Preparing for the Next Shot

A03 · Beginner · Approx. 25 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Being able to identify net shots where there is room to smash down.

Court Practice

  1. Have a partner at the net alternate between hitting 6 high shots and 4 low shots. Use a short swing to return the high shots, and let the low shots drop at the net.
  2. Qualification Criteria Correctly judge at least 8 shots, and be able to finish all net kills within your own half of the court.

Offensive Strategies / Diving for Shots at the Net

First, identify shots that can be smashed downward, then practice the forehand smash with short, concise movements to avoid hitting the net and lunging too far forward.

First, Determine Whether You Can Drive the Shuttlecock Downward

When diving for a shuttle at the net, don’t automatically hit it hard just because you see it near the net. You should only hit it downward if the shuttle’s position, the height at which you make contact, and your physical condition allow it; if the shuttle is already below the optimal position for an attack, you should instead use a net shot or a lift. Good judgment is more important than speed; avoid forcing a shot that could have extended the rally and ending up with an error.

Waiting with the Racket in Front of You

Get into your ready position early, maintain your balance as you approach the shuttlecock, and control the racket face with small movements. Since you have very little time at the net, there’s no need to swing the racket widely; after hitting the shuttlecock, be sure to bring the racket back in time to prepare for the next shot. Pay special attention to your distance from the net to avoid hitting the net with your racket or body, and don’t lunge forward to reach the shuttlecock.

Start with Fixed Feeding

Have a partner stand in a safe position and feed you easy high shots near the net. Start by practicing directing the shuttlecock into open spaces, and avoid hitting the shuttlecock forcefully toward the feeder. Play six shots per set; begin by keeping your position fixed, then add lateral movements. In the next stage, mix in lower shots and challenge yourself to choose between smashing, dropping, and lifting the shuttlecock, rather than smashing every shot.

Common Mistakes

If you frequently hit the net, shorten your swing first and check where you’re making contact with the shuttlecock; if you’re hitting the shuttlecock but it’s always going out of bounds, reduce your power and widen your target area first. If you can’t get back into position after returning a shot, check whether you’re moving too quickly or taking too big a final step. First, keep your movements within a range where you can stop and recover, then increase the pace.

Incorporating the Dive into Doubles Play

First, agree with your partner on who will play the front and back courts, and practice waiting at the net for the right opportunity. When you don’t have a good angle for a smash, it’s still valuable to keep the rally going with a solid return. After each set, reflect on whether your decisions were sound, and gradually develop the habit of assessing the shuttle’s height, deciding on your shot, and preparing for the next stroke.

Learning with BWF Videos

Key Points to Watch: Observe the contact point above the net, the short, downward stroke, and whether the racket avoids hitting the net.

After-Class Exercises: Have a partner feed you 10 shots above the net; use a short, quick stroke to return them. Then, have them feed you 10 lower shots and practice dropping the shuttlecock over the net instead.

Self-Assessment After Practice

Can you perform this set of movements consistently? What is the most common mistake? For the next set, what single aspect will you focus on adjusting? Summarize your observations in one sentence, then decide whether to increase the difficulty.

How to Return a Shot

Self-check

Your partner hits a shot that lands below the net, and you’re already at the net. How should you handle it?

First, use a steady racket face to get the shuttlecock over the net; choose to drop it at the net or play a safe lift, and get back into position. Practice net kills only when the next shot has sufficient height and space.
