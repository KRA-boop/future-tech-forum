B11 | Forehand and Backhand Net Shots

B11 · Advanced · Approx. 25 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Able to execute controlled drop shots using short strokes from both sides of the front court.

Recommended to Learn First B09 Racket Face Angle and Light Touch Control

Technique and Decision-Making

Extend your racket toward the shuttlecock early, use the racket face to meet the incoming shuttlecock, and return it over the net with a short stroke. For backhand shots in front of your body, use your thumb to provide support; for forehand shots, adjust the angle of your index finger relative to the racket face. When the shuttlecock is hit low, prioritize getting it over the net and maintaining your balance.

Court Practice

  1. Have a partner take turns tossing the shuttlecock from both sides of the net, 8 shuttles each. Start by focusing on straight shots; in the second round, add diagonal shots only to the shuttles that land in a stable position.
  2. Qualification Criteria Hit at least 5 shuttlecocks on each side into the designated front-court area and return to your position after each shot.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

Trying to hit every shot just over the net can lead to consecutive errors. Start by leaving a little extra height to clear the net, and once you’ve stabilized your game, you can adjust your shot to be lower.

Self-check

If You’re Late on a Low Shot at the Net, What Should You Keep in Mind Before Lobbing It Over the Net?

First, make sure you can maintain your balance and hit the shuttlecock over the net steadily; if necessary, switch to a higher, safer return.

Advanced Practice

When increasing the difficulty in doubles, start by practicing straight-line drop shots and straight-line returns. Once you’ve successfully executed these three times, add cross-court shots. If the shuttle is too low or arrives too late, first leave enough space to safely return it over the net. After the rally, note which side makes you lose your balance most easily.
