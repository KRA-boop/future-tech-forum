B08 | Switching Between Forehand and Backhand Grips

B08 · Beginner · Approx. 20 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

The ability to switch between forehand and backhand grips slowly in front of the body.

Technique and Decision-Making

For the forehand, grip the racket shaft in a natural grip. For backhand shots in front of you, use your thumb to support the shaft. When switching grips, loosen your fingers slightly to allow the shaft to rotate between them; the backhand grip behind your body will adjust according to the point of contact with the shuttlecock.

Court Practice

  1. Alternate hitting the shuttlecock 20 times, then have your partner toss 6 shuttlecocks to each side. Focus first on keeping the racket face facing the opposite direction; add continuity only in the third set.
  2. Qualification Criteria Out of 12 shots, hit at least 9 over the net with the correct racket face, and don’t keep your fingers clenched tightly when switching strokes.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

If you find that your entire arm is rotating, first stop your elbow in front of your body and practice moving only your fingers.

Self-check

Why Should You Relax Your Fingers After Every Return?

Relaxing creates space to change your grip and adjust the racket face for the next shot.

Advanced Practice

Use two consecutive shots as an advanced drill—the first shot with your forehand, the second with your backhand—with a brief pause between them. Once your fingers are relaxed and the racket face remains stable, shorten the interval between the two shots to practice quick grip changes.
