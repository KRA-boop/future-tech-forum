M06 | Post-Match Review Using 12 Shots

M06 · Advanced · Approx. 30 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

You can use consistent markers to identify the most common problems in rallies.

Recommended to Learn First M05—My First Match Review

Technique and Decision-Making

First, select a player to observe, and note their positioning before hitting the shuttlecock as well as the pressure applied by their opponent. Keep separate records for errors caused by pressure, errors made when the player had ample time, and points scored directly by the opponent. Review the footage a second time and refine your categorization.

Court Practice

  1. Take 12 consecutive rallies from a single game, recording the score, key shot patterns, and situations leading to point losses for each shot. Mark any shots that are difficult to judge as “To Be Confirmed.”
  2. Qualification Criteria Complete at least 10 shot markers and propose an area for improvement supported by three or more rallies.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

Treating all shots that go into the net as the same type of error can confuse the issues of shot placement and footwork. First, ask the player if they were in the correct position before hitting the shot.

Self-check

What Should You Do When You Can’t Determine the Type of Error?

First, mark it as "to be confirmed"; then review the one or two strokes leading up to the shot and your position on the court before making a judgment.

Watch Matches and Select Full Replays

Advanced Practice

After practice, organize the 12 shots into a table based on scenarios, such as “drawn to the baseline” or “unable to reach the high point at the net.” Identify the most common scenario, then find a shot you successfully handled and compare the two different stances.
