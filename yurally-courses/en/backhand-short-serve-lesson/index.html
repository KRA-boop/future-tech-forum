backhand low serve Lesson: Establishing the First Three Strokes Through Placement and Rhythm

B05 · Beginner · Approx. 25 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Able to place a backhand drop shot near the front service line.

Court Practice

  1. Serve 10 shuttles to each side of the court, aiming for the area behind the short service line. Record your wind-up for the first 3 shuttles and the last 3 shuttles.
  2. Qualification Criteria Land at least 6 shots on each side within the target area, and be ready for the third shot immediately after hitting the shuttlecock.

Class Notes · Serves and Return of Serves

From a Consistent Ready Position to a 10-Shot Streak—Develop a Stable Backhand Drop Shot and Transition to the Next Shot.

Lesson Objectives

When practicing the backhand drop shot, don’t just focus on whether the shuttlecock clears the net—also pay attention to its height, landing spot, and whether your follow-through is consistent. In this lesson, we’ll start with drills targeting specific landing spots before introducing a receiver. Aim for consistency first, and avoid compensating for an unfamiliar feel at contact by suddenly accelerating or making a sudden, forceful swing.

Key Points for Preparation and shuttle Contact

Assume a stance that allows you to maintain your balance, hold the racket in front of you, and grip the handle using the thumb grip suitable for a backhand serve. Stay relaxed during the preparatory phase, keep your swing compact, and ensure that the position of the racket head relative to the racket face is easy to replicate. After striking the shuttlecock, return the racket to a position ready for the next shot. Observe the technical details in the BWF Development instructional materials below. The legality of serves in official matches should be determined according to the applicable rules.

Ten-Shuttlecock Placement Drill

Place a safe, flat visual marker in front of the diagonal service box; avoid using objects that could cause you to trip. Serve ten consecutive shuttles, recording the number of times each shuttle clears the net, lands in the legal service box, and approaches the target area. If the shuttle frequently goes into the net, first check your racket face and the direction of the shot; if it frequently goes too high, reduce the swing amplitude and re-establish your feel for the shuttle. Adjust only one variable at a time.

Connecting the Serve to the Next Shot

Ask your partner to return the shuttlecock in only one direction at first, so you can complete your preparation and take your first step after serving. Then gradually introduce returns in other directions. This will help you check whether you relax immediately after serving or focus only on the landing spot and forget to prepare your grip. Alternate between serving and receiving drills, and exchange observations at the end of each set.

What to Look for When Watching Videos

First, observe the position of the racket and shuttlecock as the demonstrator prepares. Then, watch how the racket returns to the ready position after a short-range push and stroke. The text on this site provides practice guidelines and viewing tips; it is not a word-for-word translation of the video. You can pause the video to compare it with your own movements, so you don’t just play it continuously without paying attention to the key points.

Self-Assessment After Class

Compare the records of the two sets of ten shots—one before and one after—and observe whether the shots are landing in a more concentrated area, rather than just picking out the best shot. Once your form is consistent, increase the pressure on the receiver; if your form changes significantly as a result, lower the difficulty first. For further reading, check out our article on doubles serve and return positioning.

Self-check

My short serve success rate is already 60 percent—what variations should I try next?

First, have the player hit the shuttlecock to the same spot consistently and return to the ready position after each serve. Then, have your partner return the first shot. The goal for the next round is to be able to return the third shot.
