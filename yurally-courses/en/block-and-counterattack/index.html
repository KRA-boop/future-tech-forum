D09 | Transitioning from Defense to Offense After a Net Block

YouTube Tutorial Videos

Watch the original video on YouTube

Key Points to Watch: Watch a sequence of drills for drop shots, smashes, and net returns, and observe how to prepare for the next shot after returning a short shot.

After-Class Exercises: Have a partner feed you smashes; first return a short shot, then decide whether to move to the net or return to the center based on the height of the return; repeat 10 times, and record your decisions on when to transition from defense to offense.

D09 · Advanced · Approx. 35 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

The ability to link a defensive drop shot with the timing of the next shot when moving to the net.

Recommended to Learn First D01 Introduction to Blocking Smashes at the Net

Technique and Decision-Making

Start by using a short block to force your opponent forward, and observe whether your return has forced them into a low position. If you’ve gained the height advantage, prepare early to net the shuttlecock; if you block too high or your opponent can reach the high point, stay in a position where you can react.

Court Practice

  1. A three-player, half-speed drill. On the first shot, the attacker hits a drop shot, and the defender returns it short. On the second shot, the opponent chooses to either hit a net shot or a high lob. Complete 12 sets.
  2. Qualification Criteria Complete at least 7 sets by hitting the first shot and executing the correct direction of the second stroke.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

If you rush to the net immediately after blocking, the shuttlecock might be pushed behind you. After blocking, first check your opponent’s racket head and the height of the shot, then decide on your footwork.

Self-check

Does a Short Block Always Lead to an Attack?

Pay attention to where the shuttlecock lands and whether your opponent can reach it at its highest point. Even if your opponent reaches the shuttlecock first, you should still remain ready to defend.

Advanced Practice

During three-player rotations, have an observer note the landing spot of the second shot after the net return and the defender’s first step, then provide feedback after the rally ends. If players consistently fail at the same type of net approach, switch to a fixed shuttle feeder and practice again.
