D10 | Transitioning from Left-to-Right Defense to Front-to-Back Offense in Doubles

D10 · In-Game Application · Approx. 35 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Being able to coordinate with your partner to determine who will go for the shot and who will cover after the shuttlecock has dropped low.

Recommended to Learn First D09 Transitioning from Defense to Offense After a Net Block

Technique and Decision-Making

After one player hits a low shot, the player closest to the next intercept zone moves forward, while the other player covers the backcourt. If the return is too high or the opponent can hit it down first, maintain a defensive stance. Agree on a short code word beforehand so both players can coordinate their movements.

Court Practice

  1. Play a slow-paced four-player drill, starting with two players defending side by side. Each time the defending team successfully returns the shuttlecock low, they should call out “Forward” or “Stay.” Perform 10 role changes.
  2. Qualification Criteria Demonstrate at least 7 instances where the players' positions do not overlap, and explain the criteria for switching positions.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

If both players move forward when a drop shot is hit, they’ll leave the backcourt exposed. First, designate who has priority, then adjust your position based on where the shuttlecock lands.

Self-check

If your partner’s return is too high over the net and your opponent is at the net ready to smash it down, should you adjust your footwork first?

First, maintain your defensive position and keep the racket face in front of you; wait until you can actually hit a low shot or your opponent lifts the shuttlecock before turning.

Advanced Practice

After completing a set of drills, switch positions between offense and defense once, and observe whether you can make the same decisions on the other side. Have the four players each record an instance where their positions crossed during a rally, and practice replaying that rally using a clearer command.
