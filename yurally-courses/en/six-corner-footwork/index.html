B03 | Six-Point Footwork and Returning to Position

B03 · Advanced · Approx. 30 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Be able to switch between the front, middle, and back courts while remaining ready as you return to your position.

Recommended to Learn First B02 Step to the Net

Technique and Decision-Making

Start with small initial steps to assess the direction, keeping the racket head in the ready position as you move. When reaching the front court, come to a stop first; when reaching the back court, establish your stroke stance first, then return to a position where you can receive the next shot. Do not accelerate in a direction where you are not yet stable.

Court Practice

  1. Mark six directions on the floor. First, complete two sets following a fixed order, then have your partner randomly assign directions for two more sets. Each set lasts 30 seconds.
  2. Qualification Criteria Be able to complete the entire sequence without losing balance, and correctly perform the first step at least 8 times in a random sequence.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

When sprinting back to the center line, players often miss the right moment to start moving. Have your partner use hand signals to indicate the next shot, and focus on your partner while practicing your return to position.

Self-check

Do You Have to Step on the Center Mark When Returning to Your Position?

Anticipate where the next shot might land and where your opponent will hit it, then return to a position where you can react immediately.

Advanced Practice

After mastering the set routes, have your partner call out the direction while leaving a one-stroke gap, so you wait until you see the cue before moving. For the first set, note any mistakes in direction; for the second set, note whether you can regain your footing after returning to your position. Compare the two sets separately.
