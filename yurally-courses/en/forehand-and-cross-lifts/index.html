D04 | Forehand Lift and Diagonal Clear

D04 · Advanced · Approx. 30 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Able to hit deep forehand lifts and understand the risks of cross-court shots.

Recommended to Learn First D03 Introduction to the Lift Shot for Defense

Technique and Decision-Making

When hitting a low shot, take a step forward to stabilize your stance, keep the racket face aligned with the intended trajectory, and hit the shuttlecock high and deep. Diagonal shots travel a longer distance, so it can be difficult to hit them deep and consistently if you make contact too late; in this case, use a high, straight lift shot to get back into position.

Court Practice

  1. Have a partner feed you low forehand shots at the net—8 shots down the line and 8 cross-court. Start by focusing on landing the shots in specific zones, then add the next shot once you’ve fully returned to your position.
  2. Qualification Criteria Hit at least 5 shots to the backcourt in each direction to determine which hitting position makes it easier for you to lift the shuttlecock short.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

If you're lifting the shuttlecock but it's coming up too short, try slowing down your serve first to regain a consistent contact point and direction.

Self-check

When You're Driven to the Net and Make Contact with the Shuttlecock Very Late, Should You Test It First with a Straight Shot or a Diagonal Shot?

Start by practicing straight lifts that are easier to hit deep, and choose based on your consistency from that position.

Advanced Practice

In the second round, increase the distance between each feed by one step so you can compare the difference between arriving at the right spot too early and too late. If the shot lacks depth, first check whether your stride is stable, then examine the direction your racket is moving the shuttlecock—avoid changing too many movements at once within the same set.
