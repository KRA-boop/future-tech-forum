A10 | Organizing an Attack in Singles: Move Your Opponent First

A10 · In-Game Application · Approx. 35 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Being able to identify which shot creates an opening and knowing when to accelerate.

Recommended to Learn First A09 Backcourt Smash, Frontcourt Net Shot

Technique and Decision-Making

Start by hitting deep shots to force your opponent to turn or step back, and watch to see if there’s a pause as they return to their position. If the return is short and you’re already in position, accelerate with a straight shot or aim for an open space. If your opponent still returns the shot quickly, keep hitting deep and wait for a second opportunity; avoid forcing a shot along the sideline when you’re off-balance.

Court Practice

  1. Play 10 set rounds. On the first shot, hit the shuttlecock deep; on the second shot, watch your opponent’s recovery. Only when they’ve regained their footing and the shuttlecock is high enough should you actively drive it down.
  2. Qualification Criteria Be able to identify at least three opportunities to accelerate and your opponent's position after each rally.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

If you treat the smash as a fixed first shot, it’s easy to overlook your opponent’s position. When practicing, always leave room for the rally to continue.

Self-check

Your opponent has retreated to the backcourt, and the return is both deep and high—what should you do first?

First, move into a stable hitting position; once you’re firmly planted, decide whether to hit a drop shot or a smash. If you haven’t quite reached the right position, prioritize giving yourself time to recover.

Advanced Practice

After your partner returns each shot, have them tell you which shot made it hardest for them to get back into position. Compare your prediction with their answer. In the next round, adjust only the depth of the first shot, and observe whether the timing of the opening changes.
