Defensive Flat Drive: Handling Body Shots and Quick Forehand-Backhand Transitions

D05 · Advanced · Approx. 30 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Maintaining a front-body stroke when the shuttlecock is returned alternately from both sides.

Court Practice

  1. Have a partner alternate between serving to the forehand and backhand sides, 10 shuttles per set. Start with a fixed direction, then add one random shuttle starting with the second set.
  2. Qualification Criteria In the final set of 7 shots, you should be able to consistently return the shuttlecock over the net, with the racket head returning to near your chest after each shot.

Defensive Skills / Flat Drive Defense

Start with your stance and forehand-to-backhand transitions, and practice returning shots steadily under pressure from flat shots.

Learning Objectives for This Lesson

When defending against flat drives—which involve faster flat shots and pressure on your backhand side—the key is timely preparation, maintaining balance, and making sound decisions. This lesson begins with partner drills, then incorporates simple directional changes. Consistently returning the shuttlecock to the court and being ready for the next shot builds a stronger foundation than occasionally forcing a spectacular counter-drive.

Leave Room to React First

Keep the racket in front of your body and hold it loosely, adjusting your forehand or backhand stroke based on the incoming shot. Do not let the racket hang down by your side, and avoid using the same side of the racket to block every shot. When facing shots close to your body, shorten your stroke and maintain adequate space to move; adjust your footwork when necessary, rather than simply leaning back or swinging the racket too widely.

Segmented Practice

Two players hit flat shots in a fixed direction while maintaining a safe distance. Start by exchanging five shots, then alternate between forehand and backhand strokes. In the next phase, have your partner change the direction of the shot at a controlled speed, and practice identifying the shot’s trajectory before returning it. Keep each set short; take a break if the quality of play declines. Do not aim for the head or face, and avoid chasing the shuttle at full speed at close range.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

When you keep getting pinned by the shuttlecock, first check your ready position and stance—don’t just try to move faster. If you get stuck switching between forehand and backhand, slow down the pace and practice adjusting your grip and racket angle. If your returns keep floating high, recheck your point of contact and minimize unnecessary movements; avoid trying to compensate by hitting harder on every shot.

Transitioning from Defense to Offense

When the incoming shot and your position allow it, gradually practice returning the flat shot to an open space; if conditions aren’t ideal, focus on returning the shot consistently first. During doubles practice, coordinate with your partner to divide responsibilities in the middle of the court to avoid collisions caused by both players going for the same shot. You can combine this with a lesson on flat drives for quick attacks to compare the objectives of the same technique in offensive and defensive situations.

Learning with BWF Videos

Key Points to Watch: Observe how to handle the shuttlecock in front of you when returning a cross-court drive from the midcourt, switch grips, and stay ready.

After-Class Exercises: Have a partner alternate between feeding forehand and backhand drives; perform 3 sets of 30 seconds each; record the number of consecutive successful returns and the number of errors caused by an overly wide swing.

Self-check

When hitting consecutive flat drives, I can return them with my forehand, but my backhand is often a split-second too slow. How should I practice this next?

Slow down the pace of the feeds from both sides so you can make contact with the shuttlecock in front of you. After hitting each shuttlecock, first relax your fingers and bring the racket head back in front of you, then switch to the backhand side.
