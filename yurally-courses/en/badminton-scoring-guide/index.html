First, Understand This Topic

Modern badminton matches generally use the rally-point system, where the first player or team to reach 21 points in a game wins, provided they have a two-point lead. If the score reaches 29-29, the first player or team to score the 30th point wins the game. The official format, number of games, and other match details are subject to the specific rules of that match. When watching a match, first check the set and score to understand whether a player is playing catch-up, playing defensively, or taking greater risks.

Practice and Selection Methods

Players switch sides after each game, and they also switch sides when the deciding game reaches the specified score. The wind direction, lighting, and visual surroundings in the arena may cause the players to perceive the court differently on each side. Pay attention to whether the landing points of serves, dropshots, and clear shots change before and after the side change. Umpire announcements, challenges, and brief pauses can disrupt the flow of the match, and players will use this time to catch their breath and reassess their strategy.

Note a Detail You Can Adjust

Instead of just following the shuttlecock with your eyes, choose a single rally to observe: who takes control of the net first, which shot forces the opponent to move, and how the attacker plans the next shot. The scoreboard reflects the pressure, while the shuttlecock’s trajectory reveals the choices made; looking at both together makes it easier to understand the tactics. Self-check

Let’s say you’re watching a match at the 2026 China Open, and the score is tied at 20-20. How do you determine the outcome of this game?

In that particular tournament, the 21-point system was used; after reaching 20-20, the first team to lead by two points wins the game, and in the event of a 29-29 tie, the 30th point decides the winner. When reviewing matches from other years, be sure to check the rules in effect for that specific tournament.
