D02 | Returning a Body-Shot Smash

D02 · Advanced · Approx. 30 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

The ability to make a quick defensive move when the shuttlecock is hit toward your body.

Recommended to Learn First D01 Introduction to Blocking Smashes at the Net

Technique and Decision-Making

During your ready position, keep the racket in front of your body, with your elbow slightly away from your body. When the shuttlecock is coming straight at you, slightly adjust your center of gravity or turn your body sideways so that you make contact with the shuttlecock in front of you. When the shuttlecock is moving fast, prioritize using the momentum to block it into the safe zone; wait until you have the space and time to push it deeper.

Court Practice

  1. Have a partner feed you 6 shuttlecocks each to the center of the court and to your racket side at half speed, followed by 8 mixed shots. Start by blocking the mid-front court; once your racket face is stable, choose a different shot direction.
  2. Qualification Criteria Get at least 6 of the last 8 shots over the net, and keep the racket head in front of you most of the time.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

If your hand instinctively pulls back toward your chest when you hit a fast shot, start by practicing with slow feed shots to maintain space in front of you when making contact with the shuttlecock.

Self-check

When the shuttlecock is coming so fast that you don’t have time for a full backswing, what should you prioritize?

Prioritize stabilizing the racket face and your body position, and use short, controlled movements to generate power.

Advanced Practice

After completing the stationary drills, have the shuttle feeder alternate between attacking the sides of your body and the open spaces. After each shot, the defender should note whether they created space by moving or returned the shot by adjusting the racket face—to identify the areas they most need to improve.
