A09 | Backcourt Smash, Net Shot

YouTube Tutorial Videos

Watch the original video on YouTube

Key Points to Watch: Observe how the front-court player raises their racket, chooses the right moment to intercept, and protects the center of the court when their back-court partner drives the shuttlecock.

After-Class Exercises: With one player in the front court and the other in the back court, repeat the back-court drive and front-court intercept 10 times, then switch positions and record the reasons for any coordination errors.

A09 · Advanced · Approx. 35 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Able to work with a partner to execute backcourt smashes and frontcourt blocks.

Recommended to Learn First A02 Smash Landing Points and the Second Shot

Technique and Decision-Making

From the backcourt, hit the shuttlecock downward with enough power to maintain balance; from the frontcourt, position yourself where you can block a straight shot while leaving room to turn and cover the diagonal. In the second round, let your partner freely block either a straight or diagonal shot; both players should observe the shuttlecock’s speed and height over the net before deciding who moves forward first.

Court Practice

  1. Three-person drill. Backcourt player hits a half-speed straight-line drop shot; the player on the opposite side blocks the straight-line shot first, while the frontcourt player intercepts it. The third player blocks the diagonal shot freely. Perform a total of 12 sets.
  2. Qualification Criteria In the stationary drills, players should maintain proper positioning for at least 6 repetitions; in the directional change drills, players should call out who is responsible before a shuttle is missed.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

If a front-court player stands too close to the net, they’re likely to miss a flat drive. Step back to a position where you have time to react and can still block the net, then make slight adjustments to the landing spot based on the back-court player’s position.

Self-check

After a teammate hits a cross-court shot from the backcourt, should the player at the net still stand on the original straight line?

Reassess your opponent’s shot location and the likely return path; both players should move together to avoid leaving gaps in the middle.

Advanced Practice

Groups of four can take turns practicing two types of shots: the backcourt straight-line smash and the backcourt cross-court smash. After switching roles within each group, compare which shot makes it easiest for the frontcourt player to block the shuttle, and agree on a division of labor for returning cross-court shots.
