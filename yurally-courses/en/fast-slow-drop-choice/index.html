A06 | Fast Drop Shots and Slow Drop Shots

A06 · Advanced · Approx. 30 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

The time difference between a fast drop shot and a slow drop shot.

Recommended to Learn First A05 Introduction to Backcourt Lobs

Technique and Decision-Making

First, get into position, shift your weight to the side, and maintain a racket position similar to that used for a high clear. A fast drop shot sends the shuttlecock to the front court more quickly, while a slow drop shot brings it closer to the net; however, if it clears the net too high, it gives your opponent an opportunity to attack. Compare how these two shots affect your time to recover to your position; when you’re behind the shuttlecock, prioritize a safe landing spot.

Court Practice

  1. From the same backcourt spot, hit 6 fast drops and 6 slow drops, using two target zones in the front court to observe the height and landing point of the shuttlecock as it crosses the net.
  2. Qualification Criteria Hit at least 4 of each type of drop shot into the target area, and identify which shot you can execute more consistently.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

If your soft drop shots consistently go too high over the net, first stabilize your contact point, reduce the difference in swing speed, and then make gradual adjustments.

Self-check

Should You Keep Practicing the Same Slow Lift Shot to the Same Spot When Your Opponent Is Already Positioned at the Net?

First, adjust your shot based on your opponent’s position—try a quick drop shot or a drive to the backcourt—and see if it disrupts their movement.

Advanced Practice

Ask your partner to call out the direction and landing spot of every shot, and return to the ready position after hitting the shuttlecock. After practicing, count how many low returns each of the fast drop shot and slow drop shot produce, then choose one of them to incorporate into the rally.
