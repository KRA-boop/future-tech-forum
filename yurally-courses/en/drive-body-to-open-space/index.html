A08 | Two-Shot Placement for the Flat Drive Fast Attack

YouTube Tutorial Videos

Watch the original video on YouTube

Key Points to Watch: Learn the short motion, in-front-of-the-body contact, and racket follow-through for the forehand flat drive in doubles, and apply these techniques to two-shot direction changes.

After-Class Exercises: Perform 8 sets of two-shot drills: On the first shot, hit a flat drive next to your partner’s body; on the second shot, hit to the open space on the other side. Record the success rate of the direction changes.

A08 · Advanced · Approx. 25 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Use the first shot to restrict your opponent's swing, then change the direction of the second shot.

Recommended to Learn First A07 Introduction to the Flat Drive

Technique and Decision-Making

On your first shot, aim slightly outside your opponent’s comfort zone and watch to see if the racket head is forced toward their body; on your second shot, wait until you see an opening before changing direction. If your opponent is still able to hit the shuttlecock early in front of them, return a steady shot down the middle first to preserve the opportunity to change direction on your next shot.

Court Practice

  1. Half-court doubles flat drive. On the first shot, aim for the side of your body; on the second shot, aim for the outside based on your partner’s position. After completing 8 sets of double shots, allow free choice.
  2. Qualification Criteria Out of 8 shots, at least 5 should have distinguishable landing points on both sides of the net, and the racket should not be pulled back significantly to adjust the trajectory.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

If you pull your arm back too far before each stroke, you’ll be a split second too slow. Use an empty racket to check whether the racket head remains in front of your body at all times.

Self-check

If your opponent remains firmly on the outside after the first shot, do you have to hit to the outside on the second shot according to the practice sequence?

No need. If there’s no opening on the outside, choose the safer middle or the side of your body, and wait until the next shot to change the direction.

Advanced Practice

To increase the difficulty, have your partner return the shuttlecock with a flat drive first, then choose where to place your second shot. For each set, record only three outcomes: “inside, center, or outside.” This helps you determine whether you’re changing direction because you spotted an opening or because you made the decision in advance.
