G07 | Trying On Badminton Shoes and Adjusting Laces

G07 · Beginner · Approx. 20 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

You can check how well your shoes fit by doing a few short movements.

Recommended to Learn First G05 Choosing and Caring for Your Shoes

Technique and Decision-Making

First, check if there’s enough room for your toes while standing, then practice short-distance lateral movements and sudden stops. When tying your shoelaces, make sure the pressure is evenly distributed across the shoe, and tuck in the ends of the laces. If the fit feels different on each foot, make a note of it for each foot, and compare different sizes and last shapes.

Court Practice

  1. Wear your usual athletic socks. Start by walking straight, then make a slight sideways step—repeat each movement 3 times. After adjusting your shoelaces, perform the same movements to compare the sensation in your heel and forefoot.
  2. Qualification Criteria It can detect whether the heel is sliding or the forefoot is being squeezed, and keep a record of the appropriate shoelace tightness.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

Tightening your shoelaces doesn’t necessarily mean better stability. If you experience localized pressure or your heel slips, adjust the tightness in sections and test again.

Self-check

Does Feeling Comfortable While Running Straight Ahead Mean Your Shoes Are Right for Playing?

We also need to test lateral movement, stopping after a stride, and heel coverage.

Advanced Practice

If there’s adequate space in the toe box but your heel still slips, try adjusting the tightness of the laces or switching to a different last. Adjust only one variable at a time, then repeat the same side-to-side movements and stops, keeping track of your results before and after.
