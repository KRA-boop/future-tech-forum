Net-Play Step-In Lesson: First, Plant Your Feet Firmly; Then, Hit the Shuttlecock Over the Net

B02 · Beginner · Approx. 25 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Maintaining balance and returning to your position after hitting a shot at the net.

Court Practice

  1. Practice 6 slow shots on each side of the net. After hitting each shot, pause briefly to check your balance, then slowly connect your footwork.
  2. Qualification Criteria Out of 12 shots, at least 8 should be hit without needing to take a step to regain balance afterward.

Class Notes · Footwork and Movement

Link your start, movement, stride, and return to position, and use stationary drills to develop balanced net play.

Lesson Objectives

This lesson focuses on maintaining control of your body and racket angle while moving to and from the net. Start by simply hitting the shuttlecock over the net, then work on aiming for specific landing spots, and finally increase your speed. Before practicing, make sure the court is dry, your shoe soles are clean, and there is sufficient space to your left, right, and behind you. The following is a self-practice routine; if you feel any discomfort while taking steps, reduce the range of motion and stop forcing yourself to stretch.

Breaking Down the Movement into Four Key Points

Start from a natural ready position. When your opponent hits the shuttlecock, make a small initial move, and only start moving after you’ve assessed the shuttlecock’s trajectory. When approaching the net, take your final step with your racket-side foot, extend your arm toward the shuttlecock, and keep your torso stable. Do not lunge forward with your entire head and upper body just to reach the shuttlecock; after hitting the shuttlecock, regain your balance first, then use appropriate footwork to return to a position ready to handle the next shot. Left-handed players should reverse the left and right directions.

Stage 1—Stationary and Off-shuttle Lunge Steps

Start without a shuttlecock. Stand in a position where you can comfortably take a step forward, and perform a sequence of movements consisting of a step forward, a brief pause, and returning to your starting position. Do six repetitions per set, practicing separately on the left and right sides, with a rest between sets. Observe whether your landing is smooth, whether the direction of your toes and knees is coordinated, and whether you need to sway excessively when returning to your starting position. If you’re barely making it to the target each time by taking large strides, first shorten the distance between your starting point and the target.

Phase 2—Incorporating Hand Tosses

Ask a partner to toss the shuttlecock to a fixed spot that’s easy to reach, and practice hitting the shuttlecock in front of you and sending it over the net. Play in sets of ten shuttles, and record the number of times you can balance your stride and return to your position; this is the recording method recommended by this site and is not an official skill rating. Only after you’ve stabilized your technique should you slightly alter the landing spot—do not increase speed, distance, and unpredictability all at once.

Common Problems and Corrections

If you can’t get back into position after the shuttlecock has crossed the net, first check whether you started your movement too late or took an extra step before hitting the shuttlecock. If the shuttlecock frequently flies too high, reduce unnecessary racket swing and practice fine-tuning your racket face control. The goal of practice is to achieve consistent balance and placement; there’s no need to imitate the extreme reach of professional players.

After-Class Practice and Further Study

Use your phone to record a sequence of movements from the side to check whether the final step, point of contact, and return to position flow smoothly. Focus on adjusting only one aspect at a time. You can return to the “Footwork and Movement” section to read the article on the starting step, and then refer to the net play and stride exercises in the BWF Shuttle Time instructional materials.

Self-check

After hitting the shuttlecock at the net, you need to take another step forward to steady yourself. How can you practice this?

Start by shortening the distance from which the shuttlecock is served, and begin by taking slow steps to return the shuttlecock. Once you can stop yourself upon landing and get ready to return to your position, gradually increase the serving distance.
