A Beginner's Guide to the Smash: From the Point of Contact and Landing Spot to Preparing for the Next Shot

A01 · Beginner · Approx. 25 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

First, locate the sweet spot in the upper front part of your body, then hit the shuttlecock toward the designated area with moderate force.

Court Practice

  1. Have a partner serve you 10 high shuttles; start by aiming for straight-line targets. Each time the shuttle lands, return to your ready position before receiving the next one.
  2. Qualification Criteria Land 6 out of 10 shots in the target and maintain your balance before the next serve.

Offensive Strategies / Smash

First, practice hitting the shuttlecock downward with controlled power, then coordinate the landing spot with your return to position after the smash.

When Is the Right Time to Hit a Smash?

The purpose of the smash is to apply downward pressure, forcing a weak return or ending the rally. You should only attempt a smash when the height and position of the incoming shot, as well as your own balance, are favorable; if the shuttlecock has already landed behind you, there’s no need to force a powerful swing just to attack. In this lesson, we’ll start by practicing controlled smashes without jumping to get comfortable with the stroke and returning to your position, then gradually increase the speed.

Preparation and Key Points for Hitting the Shuttlecock

Move early to a position where you can hit the shuttlecock in front of and above you, using a relaxed forehand grip. Ensure a smooth transition between your body rotation and racket swing; avoid simply bending your wrist forcefully or gripping the racket handle too tightly. After making contact, complete the motion naturally and return to your ready position. Focus on placement and body balance rather than the sound or speed of a single shot.

Phased Practice Schedule

Start by swinging the racket without a shuttlecock to check your direction, then have a partner feed you high shots that are easy to reach. In the first set, focus only on getting the shuttlecock over the net and landing it within the court; in the second set, aim for a larger, straight-line target area; and in the third set, add smashes and return to your position afterward. Play six shots per set, take a break between sets, and switch partners to observe each other. This is the beginner’s routine on this site; you can scale it back based on your fitness level—there’s no need to start with consecutive, full-power smashes right away.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

If you frequently hit the shuttlecock into the net, check whether you’re making contact too low or hitting too forcefully in an attempt to achieve a certain angle. If the shuttlecock frequently goes out of bounds, first reduce your power and refocus on your contact point. If you lose your balance after hitting a smash, return to stationary drills to ensure you’re completing your stroke properly. If you experience discomfort in your shoulders, elbows, or wrists, stop increasing the intensity and ask your coach to help assess the issue.

Video Viewing Assignment

When watching the BWF’s “The Smash” instructional videos, pay close attention to the preparatory stance, the point of contact, and the follow-through. First, identify a specific movement you want to model; pause the video and practice the swing in slow motion to get a feel for it. Post-Class Notes

Keep track of the number of times you hit the shuttlecock in bounds, near the target, and return it to a balanced position in each set, and note the situations where you make the most mistakes. Once you’ve stabilized your form, have your partner vary the landing spots to practice deciding whether to hit a smash, a drop shot, or a clear. For further reading, check out the lesson on drop shots in the “Offensive Techniques” section to understand how to combine speed and placement.

Learning with BWF Videos

Key Points to Watch: Pay attention to the contact point—which is slightly above and in front of your body—when executing a smash, and make sure to retract your racket immediately after hitting the shuttlecock and return to your ready position.

Self-Assessment After Practice

Self-check

My smash placement is on point, but I can’t seem to return the next shot in time. What should I focus on adjusting first in the next set?

Start by reducing the downward force so you can immediately retract your racket and steady your stance after hitting the shuttlecock. Have your partner feed you a steady stream of shuttlecocks, and only increase the speed once you’ve confirmed you can return to your position consistently.
