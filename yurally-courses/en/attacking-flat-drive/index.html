Flat Drive Fast Attack: Apply Pressure Through Short Movements, Placement, and Continuous Preparation

A07 · Advanced · Approx. 30 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Maintain the speed of a flat drive by making contact with the shuttlecock near the front of the racket and using a short swing.

Court Practice

  1. Two players stand at the halfway line and perform 4 sets of straight drives, aiming for 8 strokes per set. Starting with the third set, alternate between returning the shuttlecock to the inside and outside of the body.
  2. Qualification Criteria In at least 2 out of 4 sets, reach 8 strokes, and have the racket head back in front of your body before the next shot arrives.

Offensive Strategies / Flat Drive Quick Attack

First, establish a consistent rhythm for your flat drives, then practice hitting the shuttle into open spaces—avoid simply competing to see who can swing the racket harder.

What Is the Purpose of the Flat Drive Quick Attack?

The flat drive involves a short rally; the goal is to keep the shuttlecock on a relatively flat, straight path and gain the initiative through placement and follow-through. This lesson begins with a controllable, moderate speed. Start by returning the shuttlecock continuously, then practice targeting open spaces. Speed is just one factor; whether your opponent can return the shuttlecock comfortably and whether you’re ready for the next shot are equally important.

Grip and Short-Range Control

Keep the racket in front of you where you can react quickly. Hold the racket loosely, allowing your fingers and forearm to work together to control the racket face. The shorter the rally, the less suitable it is to make wide swings. When switching between forehand and backhand strokes, focus on adjusting your grip to get into position quickly; avoid swinging your entire arm widely on every shot, as you’ll need to recover quickly and be ready for the next shot.

From Cooperation to Choice

Two players should practice flat drives in a fixed direction while maintaining a safe distance. Start by aiming for five to ten consecutive shots; once you’re comfortable, alternate between forehand and backhand strokes, then add designated open-space directions. Introduce only one variation at a time. If you make consecutive errors as soon as you pick up the pace, return to a rhythm where you can maintain quality, then gradually increase the pressure of the incoming shots.

Common Mistakes

If the shuttlecock keeps floating upward, first check your racket face and point of contact—don’t just try to compensate by hitting faster. If you frequently can’t keep up with the next shot, shorten your swing and make sure your racket returns to your front. If you’re hitting too hard and the shuttlecock often goes out of bounds, focus instead on keeping the shuttlecock in bounds and controlling its direction. Leave plenty of space in the practice area to avoid hitting the shuttlecock hard toward your partner’s head or face at close range.

Applying Them in Matches

During doubles practice, first agree on who will defend which area after a flat drive, then gradually incorporate moving to the net and dropping back. Even after hitting a fast shot, continue to watch your opponent’s return to avoid assuming you’ve already gained the upper hand. For further reading, check out the lesson on flat drive defense, which compares the purposes of the same type of flat shot in different situations.

Self-Assessment After Practice

Self-check

During a flat drive drill, the shuttlecock gets pushed right next to my body on the third stroke. How can I keep the rally going?

Start by shortening your backswing, bringing the racket head back in front of your body, and leaving space for your elbow. Perform four consecutive strokes at a slower pace; once you’ve stabilized your form, you can increase the speed.
