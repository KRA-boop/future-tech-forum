The Lift Shot for Escape: From the Backhand Lift to Gaining Time to Get Back into Position

D03 · Beginner · Approx. 25 minutes

What You'll Learn in This Lesson

When receiving a shot from the backcourt, be able to lift the shuttlecock high for a safe return.

Court Practice

  1. Start with the backhand net shot. Do 2 sets in a row, with 8 shots per set. After returning the shot, return to the designated ready position and return the next shot.
  2. Qualification Criteria Make sure at least 5 shots per set land in the backcourt, and keep your eyes on your opponent's side of the court as you return to your position.

Defensive Skills / Lob Shots for Escape

Practice hitting low shots from the front court to the back court, using height and depth to buy time to adjust your position.

The Uses and Limitations of the Lift Shot

When a shot from the front court is no longer suitable for a smash, a lift can send the shuttlecock to the back court, buying you time to get back into position. However, if the lift is too short or lacks sufficient height, it may make it easier for your opponent to attack. As a beginner, aim for the legal backcourt target zone to establish consistent shots, then gradually adjust the height, length, and direction.

Hitting the Shuttlecock in Front of You and Racket Control

Move toward the incoming shuttlecock in a balanced manner, bringing your racket to a position where you can return it in front of your body, and adjust your grip according to whether you’re using your forehand or backhand. This lesson begins with the backhand lift; avoid simply bending your wrist upward forcefully. Complete the stroke smoothly, then return to a position ready to handle the next shot—do not swing your entire body out in an attempt to hit the shuttlecock farther.

Three-Stage Practice

Start by having a partner toss the shuttlecock steadily to get a feel for the point of contact between the shuttlecock and the racket face; then incorporate stepping and returning to your position; finally, have the partner vary the direction of the toss from left to right. Play eight shuttles per set, and record the number of times each shuttlecock lands in bounds and reaches the designated backcourt area. If most land in the midcourt, slow down the pace and check your timing and movement coordination.

Common Mistakes

If the shuttlecock frequently drifts toward the sideline, check the angle of your racket face and where you’re making contact with the shuttlecock; if you lose your balance as soon as you apply force, start by shortening your stride. Don’t aim right at the baseline every time—leave yourself some safety margin. Adjust the intensity of your practice so that you can maintain your movements; if you feel any discomfort, stop increasing the workload.

BWF Viewing Tips

The "Backhand Lift" is the BWF term for the backhand lift shot. You can observe the racket grip and ready position, shuttle contact control, and follow-through. This site combines video playback instructions with practice notes on the same page to help you identify what to practice after watching—and does not present the video as the only technique suitable for everyone.

Returning a Clear and Getting Back into Position

Immediately after lifting the shuttlecock, resume scanning the court to confirm whether your opponent is in position and identify the areas you need to cover. In doubles, coordinate defensive positioning with your partner after lifting the shuttlecock; in singles, adjust your positioning based on the incoming shot and your opponent’s position. For further reading, explore the classification of high clears and positioning strategies to understand the timing and effectiveness of different backcourt returns.

Self-Assessment After Practice

Self-check

Your backhand lift often lands in the middle of the court—how should you determine the cause?

Ask your partner to watch from the side to see if you’re hitting the shuttlecock too close to your body and if you’re maintaining your balance when stepping. Focus on correcting one issue at a time; once you’ve mastered it, incorporate the return to position.
