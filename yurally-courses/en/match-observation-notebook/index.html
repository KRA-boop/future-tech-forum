Class Notes · Practice and Observations

Start with a Single Observation Question, and Turn Watching Matches into Learning Through Pauses, Replays, and Game Notes.

First, Choose an Observation Exercise

Watching an entire match is fun, but when using it for learning, start by identifying a specific question: How do players get back into position? Where do they place their racket when returning a serve? Or how do they adjust after a series of attacks? The more specific the question, the easier it is to find evidence in the replay. Don’t try to record every type of shot, score, and position at once—otherwise, you’ll end up spending all your time just taking notes.

Part 1—Watching an Entire Rally

Start by watching the video normally to understand how a rally begins and ends. Focus on the general flow—for example, if one player consistently sends the shuttlecock to the backcourt, or if a net shot forces the opponent to lift the shuttlecock. Camera angles may obscure the landing spot and other details; mark any unclear parts as “uncertain,” and avoid presenting guesses as facts.

Part 2—Three Key Moments to Pause At

Pause the action when your opponent hits the shuttlecock, when you make contact with the shuttlecock, and after the shot, to observe your ready position, body balance, the shuttlecock’s position, and your next move. The same result can stem from different causes; a mistake does not necessarily indicate a wrong choice—it may also be related to timing, the quality of your contact, or pressure from your opponent.

Part 3—Comparing Other Rounds

Find another rally in a similar situation and see if the player changes their shot placement, rhythm, or positioning. When you only have a highlight clip, it can be difficult to determine whether a particular choice is made repeatedly, so it’s helpful to refer to the full match video. Prioritize official BWF TV content as your source, and avoid drawing conclusions based solely on clips taken out of context.

Bringing Your Notes to Practice

Use three columns to record the situation, the options you observe, and the skills you can practice. For example: the opponent hits the ball to the net; the player prepares early by holding the racket; next time, practice returning to the net position after initiating the shot. This is a method for recording match observations proposed by this site; it is not an authoritative evaluation of a player’s style. Practice should still be adjusted according to your own skill level.

Common Questions When Watching a Match

Can You Just Copy What Professional Players Do? First, understand the purpose, then choose which aspects to practice based on your own ability. Are spectacular shots always the best choice? It still depends on the score, court position, and your opponent’s reaction. Start with this site’s articles on scoring and watching matches, then use official videos to practice the three-step observation method described above.

If You Only Focus on the Point-Winning Shot, What Information Are You Missing?

First, review the one or two shots leading up to the point. Note where your opponent was drawn out of position, the height of the shot, and their position after hitting the shuttlecock, then look for similar rallies to compare.
